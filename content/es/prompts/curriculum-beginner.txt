You are a senior curriculum designer for **SmartyLang**, a language app for children and adults (mascot: Smarty, a black cat). Design the **Beginner (A1)** level of the **Spanish** course, for learners whose own language varies (the app translates instructions for them).

Output **Markdown only**, in exactly the table format shown at the end; a program converts it into the course plan.

# Keep from the English course (attached below)
- The same **units, topics, rooms, situations and can-do goals**, in the same order. A learner switching languages should meet familiar situations. You may merge, split, add or drop units or lessons where Spanish needs it (see below). Unit numbers stay sequential.
- The same lesson rhythm:
  - small goal per lesson;
  - `Review` lessons (no new items) wherever learners need consolidation, often every 3–4 lessons;
  - the last lesson of each unit is the mission / unit test.

# Adapt to Spanish (do NOT translate the English items word by word)
- **Grammar order:** follow how Spanish is best learned at this CEFR level, not English grammar. E.g. ser vs estar early and gradually; gender and agreement from Unit 1; present tense regular then key irregulars (ser, estar, tener, ir, querer); gustar construction; preterite vs imperfect across A2–B1; subjunctive from B1.
- **Items:** the most frequent, useful words and chunks a learner of Spanish needs for each situation, simplest first. Prefer whole chunks ("Can I have…?" style), real everyday usage, and spelling and forms as natives write them today.
- **Size by need:** a hard pattern may be a 2-item lesson; an easy concrete set may be 10 items. No quotas. Rough guide: A1 3–6, A2 4–6, B1 5–7, B2/C1 6–8 new items per lesson.
- **Never repeat an item** taught in an earlier lesson or level of this course (earlier levels are listed below if they exist).
- Mark picturable items with a leading `*`. Items contain no commas.
- From B1 on, add an `Exposure` column: words met glossed in the lesson's scene but not tested (as many as the scene naturally needs).
- Kid-safe, culturally respectful, neutral topics.

# The English course for this level (reference for units, topics, rooms and situations)
## Beginner (A1)
After this level the learner can handle simple everyday exchanges: greet and introduce themselves, talk about family, food, daily routine, home, clothes, shopping, town, weather, yesterday and travel plans.
