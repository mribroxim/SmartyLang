.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[0].description
  Used paper is collected from homes and businesses in recycling bins and transported by trucks to a recycling centre.

.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[1].description
  Paper is sorted by type (newspaper, cardboard, office paper) and non-paper items such as plastic and staples are removed.

.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[2].description
  Sorted paper is shredded and mixed with water and chemicals in large tanks to create a wet mixture called pulp.

.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[3].description
  The pulp is filtered through fine screens to remove glue, staples, and other contaminants.

.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[4].description
  Air bubbles are injected into the pulp. Ink particles rise to the surface on the bubbles and are skimmed off.

.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[5].description
  (Optional) The pulp is bleached with hydrogen peroxide to produce bright white paper.

.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[6].description
  Clean pulp is spread onto a moving mesh screen where water drains away and fibres bond together to form a paper sheet.

.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[7].description
  The sheet passes through heated rollers to remove remaining moisture and is wound into large rolls.

.byType.process[0].chartData.stages[8].description
  Finished recycled paper rolls are cut to size and shipped to manufacturers of paper products.

.byType.process[1].chartData.stages[0].description
  Adult salmon migrate upstream from the ocean to freshwater rivers. Females lay eggs in gravel beds; males fertilise them. Most adults die after spawning.

.byType.process[1].chartData.stages[1].description
  Fertilised eggs develop inside the gravel for 6–12 weeks, depending on water temperature.

.byType.process[1].chartData.stages[2].description
  Eggs hatch into tiny fish called alevin, which remain in the gravel and absorb nutrients from an attached yolk sac.

.byType.process[1].chartData.stages[3].description
  Once the yolk sac is absorbed, the young fish (fry) emerge from the gravel and begin feeding on insects and plankton in the stream.

.byType.process[1].chartData.stages[4].description
  Over 1–3 years, fry grow into parr and develop dark markings (parr marks) that camouflage them in the river.

.byType.process[1].chartData.stages[5].description
  Parr undergo physiological changes (smoltification) to adapt to saltwater and migrate downstream toward the ocean.

.byType.process[1].chartData.stages[6].description
  Salmon spend 1–4 years at sea, growing rapidly by feeding on fish and crustaceans. They may travel thousands of kilometres.

.byType.process[1].chartData.stages[7].description
  Mature adults use their sense of smell to navigate back to their birth river to spawn, completing the life cycle.

.byType.process[2].chartData.stages[0].description
  Sunlight strikes the surface of photovoltaic (PV) cells embedded in the solar panel.

.byType.process[2].chartData.stages[1].description
  PV cells convert light energy into direct current (DC) electricity. Cells are connected in series to build up voltage.

.byType.process[2].chartData.stages[2].description
  DC electricity flows into an inverter, which converts it into alternating current (AC) electricity compatible with home appliances.

.byType.process[2].chartData.stages[3].description
  AC electricity passes through the home's fuse box and is directed to lights, sockets, and appliances throughout the building.

.byType.process[2].chartData.stages[4].description
  (Optional) Surplus electricity is stored in batteries for use at night or during cloudy periods.

.byType.process[2].chartData.stages[5].description
  Any remaining surplus is fed back into the national grid via a bidirectional smart meter, and homeowners may receive income through feed-in tariffs.

.byType.process[2].chartData.stages[6].description
  The smart meter records both electricity consumed from the grid and electricity exported, enabling accurate billing and earnings.
