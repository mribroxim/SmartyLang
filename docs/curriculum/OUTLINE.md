# Curriculum design: review and fixed outline

## Review of the first draft (what was wrong)

1. **Far too little vocabulary for C1.** It had ~2,400 items in total. A learner at B2 knows roughly 3,500–4,000 words and phrases, and at C1 roughly 5,000+ (plus many more they recognise). Teaching every word as a lesson item can't get there: it would take over 1,000 lessons. Fix:
   - teach more items per lesson as the levels go up;
   - from B1 on, add *exposure* vocabulary: words glossed (tap to translate) in scenes and stories. They're collected into the Workshop as "Found", never tested, and promoted to taught items later.
2. **The same 10 × 10 shape for every level.** A1 needs tiny steps and many short units. Higher levels need fewer, longer units. Fix: units of 5–16 lessons, sized by how much there is to learn.
3. **Gaps and wrong order in the grammar:**
   - A1 never taught question words *when/why/how*, object pronouns (*me, him, them*), articles, plurals as a point, or *was/were* and a first past simple.
   - B1 had no past perfect; it only appeared at B2.
   - B1 introduced present perfect continuous before present perfect vs past simple.
   - B2 re-taught countable/uncountable (an A2 point).
   - C1 ended with a "full review" unit that teaches nothing new.
4. **No way in for absolute beginners.** There was no sounds-and-letters start. Non-Latin scripts (Russian, Arabic, Chinese) need a script unit before Unit 1.
5. **No review lessons.** Every lesson had to add new items, so learners never got a pure practice-and-consolidate lesson. Fix: a review lesson after every 3–4 lessons, plus a unit test, plus a level checkpoint (the existing jump test).
6. **Topics repeated without going deeper.** Travel came up four times, media three times. Revisiting a topic is good (spiral learning), but each visit must do something harder: book a ticket → tell a trip story → handle a travel problem → write a travel review.
7. **Some upper topics didn't suit children** ("Law & Justice", "dictator"). Fix: keep upper levels neutral and everyday-academic, with nothing violent or political.

## Principles (Duolingo + Airlearn style)

- **Situation first:** every unit is a real-life goal ("order food", "tell a story") with a mission at the end. Grammar serves the situation and is taught as patterns, with one short tip card when needed.
- **Sized by need, never by a quota.** Nothing in the course has a fixed size: not items per lesson, not exercises, not lessons per unit, not units per level.
  - Each lesson teaches exactly what one small goal needs. A tricky pattern ("I'd like…" vs "I like…") may be a 2-item lesson with lots of practice; an easy concrete set (colours, numbers 11–20) may be 10 items in one go.
  - Exercises: as many as it takes for most learners to get ~90% right on the first try by the end, usually 3–6 minutes of practice. Easy items need few; confusing contrasts need many, in several formats.
  - Units: as long as the topic needs (a 3-lesson mini-unit is fine; so is 18).
  - The tables below are today's best estimate. Change them freely when testing shows a lesson is too heavy (split it) or too light (merge it).
  - Rough guide only (the validator warns, never blocks): A1 3–6 new items, A2 4–6, B1 5–7, B2/C1 6–8 in a typical lesson.
- **Mastery, not marching:**
  - learners who struggle get extra practice of exactly their mistakes (Workshop, Refine, retry queue);
  - learners who already know a unit can test out of it (jump tests);
  - placement puts newcomers at the right unit.
- **Constant recycling:** 30%+ of each lesson reuses older items. Every 3–4 lessons there's a **review lesson** (no new items: mixed practice, a longer scene, a mission). Every unit ends with a **unit test**.
- **Comprehensible input:**
  - A1–A2 scenes use only taught words.
  - From B1, scenes and stories may include up to 3% glossed new words (exposure vocabulary).
  - Each unit gets a short **Episode** of Smarty's story as a reward.
- **Four skills in every unit:** listen (audio first), speak (say it, mission), read (scene), write (build/type, from B1 short writing tasks). Plus a pronunciation strand in A1–A2.
- **Levels at a glance** (counts from the written lesson lists):

  | Level | Units | Lessons (reviews) | Taught items | Exposure in lessons | Running total taught |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | A1 | 12 | 110 (19) | ~410 | — | ~410 |
  | A2 | 12 | 116 (21) | ~535 | — | ~945 |
  | B1 | 12 | 140 (24) | ~660 | ~440 | ~1,600 |
  | B2 | 12 | 151 (24) | ~820 | ~690 | ~2,420 |
  | C1 | 10 | 150 (30) | ~960 | ~790 | ~3,380 |

  Taught items include many multi-word chunks, so learners actually know more words than the counts show. Recognition vocabulary also grows from three more places: exposure words in scenes, each unit's Episode (a longer reward story, ~150–600 words from B1), and items promoted from exposure into later lessons. With these, a C1 graduate meets roughly 6,000+ words and phrases.

## Rules for writing each lesson list
- Every unit: lessons in order, **review lessons marked `Review`** (no new items) wherever learners need consolidation (often every 3–4 lessons), and the last lesson is the **mission/unit test**.
- Size every lesson, exercise set and unit by what the learner needs (see "Sized by need"); there are no quotas.
- Items are exact words or chunks, with no commas inside an item. Mark picturable items with `*`.
- No item is taught twice across the whole course; reuse goes in recycle.
- B1+ lessons may also list **exposure words** (glossed in scenes, not tested) in an `Exposure` column, as many as the scene naturally uses (keep scenes ≥97% understandable).
